# Resource Library

`Bug Bounty` · `Stage 5 — Reporting & Career` · `Level: All` · `Reading time: ~8 min`

The problem with bug bounty resources isn't finding them — it's that there are thousands. This page is a **curated map**, organised by what you're trying to do, not an exhaustive dump.

<Admonition type="tip" title="How to use this">

Don't try to consume everything. Pick one item per row that matches your current stage, go deep, then come back. Breadth here is a trap; depth is the goal.

</Admonition>

## Learn From Real Bugs (Write-ups)

Reading disclosed reports is one of the fastest ways to improve.

| Source | What you get |
|---|---|
| **HackerOne Hacktivity** | Disclosed real reports, searchable by type |
| **Pentester Land / write-up aggregators** | Curated feeds of hunter write-ups |
| **Personal blogs & Medium** | Deep dives on specific bugs and chains |
| **Program disclosure pages** | How specific targets fail |
| **Conference talks** (YouTube) | Methodology and novel techniques |

<Admonition type="info" title="Pro lane: read for method, not payloads">

When reading a write-up, don't just copy the payload — extract the **thought process**: Why did they look there? What made them suspicious? How did they escalate? The transferable skill is the reasoning, not the specific bug (which is probably patched).

</Admonition>

## Cutting-Edge Research

| Source | What it's for |
|---|---|
| **PortSwigger Research** (incl. "Top 10 Web Hacking Techniques" each year) | The most important new techniques, curated annually |
| **Vendor security advisories & CISA KEV** | Fresh CVEs to hunt (1-day) |
| This site's **[CVE Radar](/cve-radar)** | Track new CVEs against your recon inventory |
| Security conference proceedings (Black Hat, DEF CON, etc.) | Deep novel research |

<Admonition type="info" title="2025–2026 technique directions">

Recent top techniques trended toward **parser differentials**, **protocol-level and side-channel attacks** (HTTP/2 tricks, cross-origin leaks via timing/size oracles), **Unicode normalization abuse**, **ORM/search-feature data leaks**, **novel SSRF** (redirect-loop techniques), and **error-based blind SSTI**. The pattern: when the obvious classes are well-defended, bugs move into *how systems interpret and parse* data, and into *measurable differences* (timing, size, errors). Worth studying once you're past the fundamentals.

</Admonition>

## A Note on Keeping Current

Tools, techniques and even platform policies change fast (the AI-report situation in 2025–2026 is a vivid example). Favour:

- **Primary sources** (PortSwigger, OWASP, vendor advisories) over second-hand summaries,
- **Recent** write-ups and research for techniques,
- **Timeless** books and labs for fundamentals.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Pick one resource per job and go deep; don't drown in breadth.
- Labs for fundamentals, methodology repos for technique, write-ups for reasoning, research for the edge.
- Read write-ups for the thought process, not just the payload.
- Favour primary and recent sources for anything fast-moving.
