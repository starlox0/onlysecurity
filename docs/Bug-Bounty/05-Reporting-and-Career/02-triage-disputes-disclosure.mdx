# Triage, Disputes & Disclosure

`Bug Bounty` · `Stage 5 — Reporting & Career` · `Level: L1 → L5` · `Reading time: ~9 min`

Submitting is the start, not the end. How you handle triage, disagreements and disclosure shapes your reputation as much as your findings do.

---

## The Report Lifecycle

```text
Submitted ──► Triaged (validity/severity confirmed) ──► Accepted ──► Fixed ──► Rewarded ──► Resolved
      │              │
      │              └─► Need more info ──► you respond
      ▼
 Closed as: Duplicate / Informative / N/A / Out of scope / Not applicable
```

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **New / Triaging** | Program is reviewing; may ask for more info |
| **Triaged / Accepted** | Confirmed valid; severity set; often when reward is decided |
| **Duplicate** | Someone reported it first |
| **Informative** | Valid observation, insufficient impact to reward |
| **N/A** | Not a valid security issue, or out of scope |
| **Resolved** | Fixed and closed |

---

## Response Times and Patience

Programs vary from hours to months. Many platforms publish average response times — a factor in **[choosing programs](../01-Foundations/03-platforms-and-programs.mdx)**. While waiting:

- Don't spam follow-ups; one polite nudge after a reasonable period is fine.
- Don't retest aggressively or escalate on your own.
- Move on to other hunting — never block on one report.

<Admonition type="tip" title="Beginner lane">

A slow response is normal, not a rejection. Keep hunting. Hunters who stake their mood on one pending report burn out; hunters with a pipeline of work stay steady.

</Admonition>

## Handling Duplicates

Duplicates sting, but they're part of the game (only the first valid report pays).

- **Verify** it's genuinely a duplicate if the program discloses enough to tell; politely ask if unclear.
- **Learn:** duplicates on easy bugs mean you're hunting where everyone else is. Shift toward newer assets, deeper logic, and less-contested surfaces.
- Don't take it personally or accuse the program of lying — reputable programs honour genuine firsts.

---

## Handling "Informative" / "N/A"

If you believe a finding has more impact than the triager saw:

1. **Re-read the scope and excluded issues** — make sure you're right before disputing.
2. **Add impact, not emotion:** demonstrate a concrete attack, ideally a chain, that raises severity. New evidence reopens discussions; repetition doesn't.
3. **Be specific:** "Here's a PoC showing this affects any user without interaction" beats "I think this deserves more."

<Admonition type="warning" title="Disagree professionally">

Triagers are people, often handling huge volumes. Rudeness, threats or public complaints damage your reputation across the platform and rarely change a decision. Make your case once, clearly, with evidence — then accept the outcome gracefully. Your long-term access depends on being someone programs want to work with.

</Admonition>

---

## Mediation

Most platforms offer **mediation** when a hunter and program can't agree. Use it as a last resort, with evidence, after a genuine attempt to resolve things directly. It exists for real disputes, not for every downgrade.

---

## Rewards, Taxes and Records

- Rewards may be money, swag, points/reputation, or thanks (VDPs).
- **Bounty income is usually taxable** — keep records of payouts, and understand your local obligations (you're typically self-employed/independent for this income). This isn't tax advice; consult a professional for your situation.
- Keep your own log of submissions, outcomes and payments — useful for taxes, for CVs, and for understanding which strategies pay.

## Responsible Disclosure

Public write-ups build your reputation and teach others — but only under the right conditions.

```text
Fix shipped  +  program permits disclosure  +  (coordinated timeline respected)
        │
        ▼
Publish: redact sensitive details, help defenders, credit fairly
```

Rules:

- **Only disclose if the program's policy allows it**, and follow any required process/timeline.
- **Never disclose before a fix** unless explicitly permitted.
- **Redact** anything that could harm the company or users (real data, exact internal hostnames where sensitive).
- **Never** use disclosure as leverage for payment — that's extortion, not research.
- Credit co-researchers and the program fairly.

<Admonition type="info" title="Pro lane">

Good public write-ups are a career engine: they demonstrate skill to programs and employers, attract private invites, and contribute to the community whose repositories and research you learned from. Many hunters' first job offers and live-event invites came from a well-received write-up.

</Admonition>

---

## Key Takeaways

- Know the lifecycle; respond promptly to info requests and be patient otherwise.
- Duplicates and "informative" closures are signals to hunt deeper and argue impact with evidence.
- Dispute professionally and sparingly; use mediation as a last resort.
- Disclose only when permitted, after a fix, redacted, and never as leverage.
