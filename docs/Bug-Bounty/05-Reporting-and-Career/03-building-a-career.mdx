# Building a Sustainable Career

`Bug Bounty` · `Stage 5 — Reporting & Career` · `Level: L2 → L5` · `Reading time: ~11 min`

Most people who try bug bounty quit. The ones who last treat it like a craft: they manage their energy, specialise, measure what works, and build a reputation that compounds. This page is about longevity.

---

## The Income Reality

Be honest with yourself about money so you don't make bad decisions:

- Income is **lumpy and unpredictable** — long dry spells, then several findings at once.
- Beginners often earn little for months while learning. That's tuition, not failure.
- Very few people earn a full-time living from public programs alone; most top earners rely on **private programs, live events, and specialisation**.
- Platform statistics consistently show a **small fraction of hunters earn the large majority** of rewards — usually the specialists and the consistent.

<Admonition type="warning" title="Don't quit your day job on a hope">

Treat bug bounty as a skill to build **alongside** income at first. Go full-time only when you have a consistent, proven track record and a financial cushion — not after one lucky critical. The unpredictability is real.

</Admonition>

---

## Specialise to Stand Out

Once you're past the basics, **depth beats breadth**. Pick an area and become genuinely excellent at it:

| Specialisation | Why it pays |
|---|---|
| A bug class (OAuth, SSRF, cache, request smuggling, deserialization) | You find what generalists miss |
| A technology (GraphQL, Salesforce, SAP, a specific CMS, mobile, Web3) | Fewer hunters have the depth |
| A target type (fintech, SaaS multi-tenant, APIs) | Transferable logic knowledge across similar programs |
| Source review / 1-day hunting | Deep, less-contested findings |

Specialists get into private programs and live events, and are the ones companies hire.

---

## Consistency Beats Intensity

- A steady **weekly routine** (e.g. a few hunting sessions + learning + reading write-ups) outperforms occasional marathons.
- Keep a **pipeline**: several programs/targets in progress so one slow report doesn't stall you.
- **Measure**: track hours vs results per program and per strategy. Double down on what works *for you*.
- Review your `ideas.md` regularly — new skills unlock old ideas.

<Admonition type="tip" title="Beginner lane">

Set process goals, not outcome goals. "Hunt 5 hours this week and read 10 write-ups" is in your control; "find a critical" is not. Process goals keep you going through dry spells — which is when most people quit.

</Admonition>

---

## Avoiding Burnout

Bug bounty is uniquely draining: competitive, often unrewarded effort, easy to compare yourself to others.

- Expect and **plan for dry spells** — they're normal, not a verdict on your ability.
- **Take real breaks.** Stepping away often *causes* the breakthrough (fresh eyes).
- Don't doomscroll other hunters' big payouts; you see their wins, not their months of nothing.
- Protect sleep, movement and life outside hunting. Sustainable beats intense.
- Celebrate process wins (learned a new class, shipped a clean report), not just payouts.

---

## Learn in Public & Collaborate

- **Write-ups** (when disclosure is allowed) build reputation and cement your own learning.
- **Collaboration/duos:** many hunters team up and split bounties — combining recon strength with exploitation strength, or two specialisations. Agree on splits up front.
- Engage with the community (respectfully): learning from others' methodology and research is how everyone, including you, improves.
- Give back: the community repositories and research you learned from were written by hunters who shared.

---

## Live Hacking Events

Platforms run invite-only, time-boxed events (often in person) with boosted rewards and collaboration.

- Invitations come from **consistent performance and signal**.
- They're intense, social, and a huge accelerator — networking, learning, and concentrated earning.
- A realistic medium-term goal once you're consistent: perform well enough on a platform to get invited.

---

## Where Bug Bounty Leads

Bug bounty is a skill set with many exits — you're not limited to hunting forever:

| Path | Fit |
|---|---|
| **Full-time hunter** | Consistent high performers with risk tolerance |
| **Penetration tester / red teamer** | Structured testing for a firm or in-house |
| **Application security engineer** | Building defences, reviewing code, running the program from the other side |
| **Security researcher** | Novel techniques, tooling, publishing |
| **Triage / platform roles** | Working at a bounty platform |
| **Founder / tooling** | Building security products (many popular tools started as a hunter's side project) |

A strong bug bounty track record — valid reports, write-ups, CVEs, event participation — is a compelling portfolio for all of these.

<Admonition type="info" title="Pro lane">

Your **reputation is the compounding asset**. Valid reports, fair conduct, good write-ups and responsible disclosure build trust that brings private invites, event slots, collaboration offers and job interviews. Protect it: no slop, no drama, no extortion, no out-of-scope testing. The hunters who last are the ones programs and peers want to keep working with.

</Admonition>

---

## Key Takeaways

- Income is lumpy; build the skill alongside stability and go full-time only on a proven track record.
- Specialise — depth in a niche is what gets you into private programs and events.
- Consistency, measurement and burnout management decide who lasts.
- Bug bounty opens doors to pentesting, AppSec, research and more; your reputation is the asset that compounds.
