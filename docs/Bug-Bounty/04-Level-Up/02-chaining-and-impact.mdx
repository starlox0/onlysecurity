# Chaining & Impact

`Bug Bounty` · `Stage 4 — Level Up` · `Level: L3 → L5` · `Reading time: ~11 min`

The difference between a hunter who earns pocket money and one who earns a living is usually **impact**. The same raw finding can be "informative" or "critical" depending on what you chain it into and how clearly you demonstrate what an attacker could actually do.

```text
Low bug  +  Low bug  +  Low bug   ──►   One high/critical chain
(open redirect) (info leak) (CSRF)      (account takeover)
```

---

## Why Chaining Pays

- A single open redirect: often **low** or N/A.
- Open redirect used to steal an OAuth `code`: **account takeover** — high/critical.

Programs pay for the **outcome**, not the ingredients. Your job is to find the outcome the ingredients enable.

---

## The Impact Ladder

For any finding, push it up this ladder before reporting:

```text
"Something is odd"                      (not yet a report)
  ↓ what can I read/change with it?
"I can see/do X on my own account"      (low–medium)
  ↓ can it affect another user?
"I can do X to another user"            (medium–high)
  ↓ without their interaction? at scale? to admins?
"I can do X to any user, unauthenticated, at scale"   (critical)
```

Always ask: **to whom, without what interaction, at what scale, affecting what data/money?**

---

## Classic Chains

| Chain | Result |
|---|---|
| IDOR on "change email" → trigger password reset | Account takeover |
| Host-header injection → reset link to attacker host | Account takeover |
| Open redirect → steal OAuth `code`/token | Account takeover |
| Self-XSS + login/logout CSRF → force-login victim into your account, then your XSS runs in their context | XSS on victim → ATO |
| Stored XSS in user content → fires in admin panel | Admin compromise |
| Mass assignment `emailVerified:true` → skip verification → claim pre-registered account | Takeover |
| SSRF → cloud metadata → temporary credentials | Cloud/internal compromise |
| CORS misconfig (reflected origin + credentials) → read API responses cross-origin | Data theft |
| Subdomain takeover on `*.target.com` → cookie scoped to parent → session theft | ATO / data theft |
| Rate-limit bypass → brute-force OTP → 2FA bypass | Auth bypass |
| Blind SSRF + redirect trick → reach internal service | Internal access |
| LFI → log poisoning → RCE | Server compromise |

<Admonition type="tip" title="Beginner lane">

Keep a running list of your "weak" findings on a target in `ideas.md` (open redirects, info leaks, CSRFs, self-XSS, low IDORs). Before moving on, spend 30 minutes asking "can any two of these combine?" Many first high-severity reports come from connecting two things you already found.

</Admonition>

## Turning Self-XSS Into Real XSS

Self-XSS (you can only inject into your own view) is normally worthless — but it becomes real if you can make a **victim's browser run as you**:

- **Login/Logout CSRF:** if login lacks CSRF protection, an attacker page can silently log the victim into the *attacker's* account, where the self-XSS payload is stored, which then runs in the victim's browser — and can pivot.
- Combined with a flaw that moves data between accounts, self-XSS can reach the victim's real session.

Report the *chain's* impact, not the self-XSS in isolation.

---

## Demonstrating Impact Without Harm

Impact must be **shown**, but safely:

- Use **two accounts you own** to prove cross-user impact.
- For "any user at scale," demonstrate the mechanism on your own objects and **explain** (don't execute) how it generalises.
- Record a short video or step-by-step request log of the full chain.
- Never touch real users' data to "prove more impact" — describing the obvious generalisation is enough and expected.

<Admonition type="warning" title="More impact is not a licence to go further">

Once you've proven a vulnerability lets you reach other users' data, **stop**. Pulling real records "to show scale" can void safe harbor and harm people. Triagers understand that an IDOR with sequential IDs affects everyone — you don't need to prove it by accessing everyone.

</Admonition>

---

## Arguing Severity in the Report

Make the triager's job easy:

1. **State the outcome first:** "Any user's account can be fully taken over without interaction."
2. **Map the chain:** step 1 → step 2 → step 3, each with the exact request.
3. **Name the impact dimensions:** who (any user), interaction (none), scale (all accounts), data (PII + funds).
4. **Suggest a CVSS vector** if the program uses CVSS, with brief justification.
5. **Pre-empt downgrades:** if part of the chain might be seen as low, explain why the *combination* is high.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Programs pay for outcomes; chain weak findings into real impact before reporting.
- Push every finding up the impact ladder: to whom, what interaction, what scale, what data.
- Self-XSS, open redirects, CSRF and info leaks are powerful chain ingredients.
- Demonstrate impact with your own accounts, then stop — don't harm real users to "prove scale."
