# Platforms & Choosing Your First Program

`Bug Bounty` · `Stage 1 — Foundations` · `Level: L0 → L3` · `Reading time: ~11 min`

Picking **where** to hunt matters almost as much as **how** you hunt. The wrong program — tiny scope, thousands of hunters, slow payouts — can make a good hunter look like a bad one.

---

## The Main Platforms

| Platform | Known for |
|---|---|
| **HackerOne** | The largest platform; many big-brand public and private programs; public "Hacktivity" feed of disclosed reports |
| **Bugcrowd** | Large platform; uses the open **Vulnerability Rating Taxonomy (VRT)** for severity; many private programs |
| **Intigriti** | Strong European presence; regular community challenges |
| **YesWeHack** | European platform with many public-sector and EU programs |
| **Immunefi** | Web3 and smart contracts; some of the largest individual payouts in the industry |
| **HackenProof** | Web3-focused plus traditional programs |
| **Synack** | Vetted, invite-only researcher network with an application process |

### Self-hosted programs

Many large companies run their own programs outside platforms — for example **Google** (Vulnerability Reward Program), **Microsoft** (MSRC), **Apple** (Apple Security Bounty), **Meta**, and many others. These often have huge scopes and well-documented rules, and some publish their own write-up and research guides.

<Admonition type="tip" title="Beginner lane">

You don't need to be on every platform. Start on **one or two**, learn their interface and rules, and build reputation there. Reputation and signal on a platform are what unlock private invites.

</Admonition>

## How Reputation and Invites Work

Platforms track your history. Exact formulas differ, but the ideas are the same everywhere:

| Metric | What it measures | How to improve it |
|---|---|---|
| **Reputation / points** | Total value of your valid findings over time | Find valid bugs, especially higher severities |
| **Signal / accuracy** | The proportion of your reports that are valid | Don't submit out-of-scope, excluded or unverified reports |
| **Impact** | Average severity of your valid reports | Escalate and chain before reporting |

Low-quality reports (N/A, spam, out of scope) **lower** your signal and can cost you invites or even get you restricted. One verified, well-written medium beats five speculative "possible" reports.

## Reading a Program Like a Hunter

Before choosing a program, look at:

| Signal | Good sign | Warning sign |
|---|---|---|
| **Scope size** | Wildcards (`*.example.com`), many apps, APIs, mobile apps | One static marketing site |
| **Response efficiency** | Fast first response and triage times (shown on many platforms) | Reports open for months |
| **Reward table** | Clear ranges, decent lows and mediums | Only criticals pay meaningfully |
| **Resolved reports** | Steady flow of resolved reports | Hundreds of hunters, very few resolutions |
| **Launch date** | Recently launched or recently expanded scope | Years old, heavily picked over |
| **Product complexity** | Many features, roles, integrations, payments | Few features, little user interaction |
| **Disclosure** | Disclosed reports to learn from | No information at all |

## Wide vs Deep: Two Valid Strategies

```text
WIDE (recon-heavy)                        DEEP (app-heavy)
───────────────────────────               ───────────────────────────
Huge wildcard scopes                      One complex application
Find forgotten hosts and assets           Understand every feature and role
Misconfigs, takeovers, exposed panels     IDOR, logic, auth, chained bugs
Benefits from automation                  Benefits from patience and notes
Competes on speed                         Competes on understanding
```

<Admonition type="info" title="Pro lane">

Most professionals combine both: wide recon to **find** the interesting, less-tested corners of a target, then deep manual testing **inside** those corners. A forgotten internal tool on an obscure subdomain, tested deeply, is where many critical findings come from.

</Admonition>

## Choosing Your First Program — a Practical Method

1. **Filter for scope:** look for wildcard domains or several web apps and APIs.
2. **Filter for activity:** prefer programs with good response times and regular resolved reports.
3. **Prefer products you understand:** if you've used the product (a SaaS tool, an e-commerce site), you already know its features.
4. **Prefer complexity:** user roles, teams/organisations, invitations, file uploads, payments, integrations — each is a bug surface.
5. **Commit:** spend at least **2–4 weeks** on one program before judging it. Hopping between programs every day keeps you at surface level, where all the duplicates are.

### Good first-target features

- Organisations/teams with multiple roles (admin, member, viewer) → access control bugs.
- Invitations and sharing → IDOR, privilege escalation, account takeover.
- Profile and settings → IDOR, mass assignment, CSRF, XSS.
- Exports, reports and PDF generation → IDOR, SSRF, injection.
- Integrations and webhooks → SSRF.
- Billing and coupons → business logic, race conditions.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Start on one or two platforms; protect your signal from day one.
- Choose programs with big scope, good response times and complex features.
- Pick wide, deep, or a mix — but commit to a program long enough to understand it.
- Track your time and results so your strategy improves with data, not mood.
